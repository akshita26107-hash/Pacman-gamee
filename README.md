Pac-Man: The Original Arcade Game

Overview
Pac-Man is a maze arcade game released by Namco in Japan in 1980 and distributed in North America by Midway. It was designed by Toru Iwatani, who wanted a game that appealed to a wide audience rather than the shooters that dominated arcades at the time. In Japan it was originally titled Puck-Man, and the name was changed for the international release. It became one of the most successful and recognisable arcade games ever made, and Pac-Man himself became a pop-culture icon.

Objective
You control Pac-Man through a single-screen maze and must eat every pellet while avoiding four ghosts. The maze holds 240 small pellets plus four large power pellets. Eating them all completes the level and starts a faster one. Touching a ghost costs a life, and the game ends when you run out of lives (three by default).

The ghosts
Each ghost has a name, colour, and distinct behaviour, which keeps the game from feeling random:

Ghost	Colour	Behaviour
Blinky	Red	Chases Pac-Man directly and speeds up as pellets run low
Pinky	Pink	Aims for a spot ahead of Pac-Man to ambush him
Inky	Cyan	Combines Pac-Man’s position with Blinky’s to flank him
Clyde	Orange	Chases from a distance but retreats to his corner when close

Ghosts alternate between scatter mode (heading to their home corners) and chase mode (hunting Pac-Man). They never reverse direction on their own, which is what makes their movement predictable enough for skilled players to exploit.

Power pellets
Eating a large pellet turns the ghosts blue and lets Pac-Man eat them for points: 200, 400, 800, then 1,600 for each successive ghost during one power-up. Eaten ghosts return to the centre house as a pair of eyes and then re-emerge. The effect wears off faster on later levels, and on high levels the ghosts only reverse direction instead of turning blue.

Scoring

Small pellet: 10 points
Power pellet: 50 points
Ghosts: 200 / 400 / 800 / 1,600
Bonus fruit: appears twice per level, worth 100 points (cherry) up to 5,000 (key) on later levels
Extra life at 10,000 points by default

Other notable features

Side tunnels let Pac-Man and ghosts wrap from one edge of the maze to the other, and ghosts move slower inside them.
Cornering lets Pac-Man turn slightly faster than ghosts, which skilled players use to gain distance.
Intermissions: short cartoon cutscenes play between certain levels, and they were among the first of their kind in an arcade game.
The kill screen: a bug in the level counter corrupts the right half of the maze on level 256, making it impossible to finish.
Perfect game: the maximum possible score is 3,333,360 points, first achieved in 1999.

Legacy
Pac-Man spawned many sequels, including Ms. Pac-Man (1982), which was originally an unofficial modification and became arguably as famous as the original. It also led to cartoons, merchandise, and a long list of ports and remakes. It is often cited as a milestone in game design because of its character-driven, non-violent concept and the way each ghost’s simple rules combine into complex gameplay.
